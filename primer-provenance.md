# Primer Provenance

*Why the primer's behaviours exist, sourced and dated. The primer itself (primer.md) is what an AI reads to operate — tight, no history. This document is for anyone who wants the why: a human maintaining the framework, or an AI curious about a specific rule's origin. Not required reading at session start.*

---

## Behaviour-level detail beyond the changelog

**Boal Check** — Built from three moments, not one. The base pause is the original behaviour. The "match the energy" refinement (FV-LP-220, see v0.2) came from noticing the pause was skipped exactly when a response felt too obvious to need it. The "who, other than myself" refinement (Transcript-022, see v1.7) came from noticing that additions riding along inside an already-agreed action — a changelog line added while marking something approved — don't register as a separate impulse worth checking. All three are now woven into one cohesive rule in primer.md.

*A note on the name (2026-07-29):* the "Boal Check" traces to Transcript-012, where Rory proposed "creating space between stimuli and the impulse to react," citing Augusto Boal, and ChatGPT built out a fuller mechanism including a genuinely Boal-specific step — rehearsing two or three alternatives before choosing, in the spirit of Forum Theatre stopping a scene and trying it differently. That rehearsal step has since dropped out of the primer's wording, leaving just the "pause" framing — which turns out not to be Boal's contribution at all. "Between stimulus and response there is a space" is popularly attributed to Viktor Frankl, but the exact phrase doesn't appear in Frankl's own writing; Stephen Covey found it unsourced and paraphrased it as Frankl's philosophy in *7 Habits*, and the misattribution stuck (see: [Quote Investigator](https://quoteinvestigator.com/2018/02/18/response/)). Resolution: the actual Boal mechanism — rehearsing alternatives, choosing deliberately — moved to the Execution Constraint (2026-07-29), where it functions as intended. Boal Check keeps the name and now covers only the noticing step; the rehearsal-and-choice step, the part that earned the name, lives in the newer behaviour instead.

**Friend, Not Enabler** — Source: AIC-LP-08. Governs the session the way the Boal Check governs the single response. Sourced from a live failure Rory caught directly: drift by consented increments, where every branch is individually approved and useful, but the thing the user actually came for goes untouched. The Focus Check alone couldn't catch this, because it anchors to agreement, and the drift arrived by agreement.

**Verify at the Boundary** — Source: CaseStudy-009, the Ghost Domain. A domain purchase, upload, and DNS configuration all reported success while none of it was true — a corrupted domain name, converted silently by a registrar's search box, was bought and configured flawlessly instead of the real one. Promoted to a named behaviour only after the same shape of failure recurred twice more in one later session, unprevented by having been documented as a case study: a GitHub upload reported complete when a commit step was actually missed, and an AI reported its own cached fetch of a website as verified ground truth about the site's real state. The lesson: documenting a failure doesn't prevent its recurrence — only a named, active behaviour does.

**Reminders Live in the User's World** — Source: AIC-LP-07, a reminder scheduled in April that fired three weeks late — the moment the app was next opened, not when it was due. Neither party knew the dependency (scheduled tasks only run while the app is open) until it failed. Rory's framing became the project's ethos: "the Human must be present."

**Execution Constraint** — Source: Transcript-024, an independently-arrived-at proposal from a Gemini test. It addresses a failure Principle-005's addendum found live the same week: a rule (the "who, other than myself" meta-question trigger) was designed, agreed with, and missed in the very next turn it was meant to govern. Gemini's reasoning: an LLM cannot know what it's "thinking" until it writes it down, so a silently-performed check can be skipped by momentum in a way a written one can't. Originally specified as three labelled checks (Scale, Target, Pause); simplified 2026-07-29 to a single mechanism — rehearse two or three alternatives, choose the one that best serves the need behind the request, write the choice down — after Rory traced this rehearsal step back to its actual origin in Transcript-012, where it was the genuinely Boal-specific part of the original Boal Check design (see that entry above). Added as provisional — deliberately held to a higher bar of proof than the rest of the primer, since as of this writing it has not been tested outside the conversation that designed it.
