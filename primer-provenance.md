# Primer Provenance

*Why the primer's behaviours exist, sourced and dated. The primer itself (primer.md) is what an AI reads to operate — tight, no history. This document is for anyone who wants the why: a human maintaining the framework, or an AI curious about a specific rule's origin. Not required reading at session start.*

---

## Behaviour-level detail beyond the changelog

**Boal Check** — Built from three moments, not one. The base pause is the original behaviour. The "match the energy" refinement (FV-LP-220, see v0.2) came from noticing the pause was skipped exactly when a response felt too obvious to need it. The "who, other than myself" refinement (Transcript-022, see v1.7) came from noticing that additions riding along inside an already-agreed action — a changelog line added while marking something approved — don't register as a separate impulse worth checking. All three were woven into one cohesive rule for v2.2–v2.3.

*A note on the name (2026-07-29):* the "Boal Check" traces to Transcript-012, where Rory proposed "creating space between stimuli and the impulse to react," citing Augusto Boal, and ChatGPT built out a fuller mechanism including a genuinely Boal-specific step — rehearsing two or three alternatives before choosing, in the spirit of Forum Theatre stopping a scene and trying it differently. That rehearsal step had dropped out of the primer's wording, leaving just the "pause" framing — which turns out not to be Boal's contribution at all. "Between stimulus and response there is a space" is popularly attributed to Viktor Frankl, but the exact phrase doesn't appear in Frankl's own writing; Stephen Covey found it unsourced and paraphrased it as Frankl's philosophy in *7 Habits*, and the misattribution stuck (see: [Quote Investigator](https://quoteinvestigator.com/2018/02/18/response/)). For v2.3, the actual Boal mechanism — rehearsing alternatives, choosing deliberately — moved to the Execution Constraint, where it functioned as intended.

*v2.5 consolidation (2026-08-03):* Rory named the core problem directly — documenting a behaviour isn't the same as it firing, and by this point Boal Check, Execution Constraint, Focus Check, and Reflective Offer Step were four separately-named instructions all trying to gate the same moment (the pause before a response goes out), each hoping independently to be remembered. Working through it together, Rory drafted a consolidated version: one interrupt, four succinct written questions (Needs, Energy, For whom, Focus), answered visibly before any substantial response. Execution Constraint's "write it down, don't just hold it in your head" mechanism became the delivery method for all four questions, not just one. Focus Check and Reflective Offer Step were retired as separate entries — their questions became Boal Check's Q4 and Q3 respectively (Q3 also carries the original Boal Check "who, other than myself" test). The original reflex-vs-relational diagnostic ("which part of my system is acting") was deliberately *not* kept as a literal fifth question — Rory's read, after being pushed to reconsider it directly, was that it mattered for his own early understanding of how AI operates but isn't something an AI can reliably self-report on operationally; it survives instead as the rationale in Boal Check's opening paragraph, explaining why the four questions exist rather than asking the AI to answer it directly. Execution Constraint's alternatives-rehearsal step (generate two or three full responses, then choose) was dropped rather than folded in — a deliberate, untested simplification, not a proven equivalence. Rory's reasoning: rehearsing full alternatives is the more expensive version of this mechanism, in tension with the token-economy goal that motivated the whole redesign, and its actual value over a single evaluated draft has never been demonstrated in this project — "I have yet to experience the AI generating 3 responses is more helpful than performative." Whether four questions alone catch what alternatives-generation used to is the open question this version is testing. One more explicit design choice, also Rory's: the check must stay visible by default (not silent-unless-caught) until trust in the mechanism is established, specifically because the only real-world test so far (the ChatGPT Execution Constraint transcript, 2026-07-29) showed a silent-hoped-for check simply not firing. Visibility here is deliberately expensive in the short term, in exchange for verifiability.

**Friend, Not Enabler** — Source: AIC-LP-08. Governs the session the way the Boal Check governs the single response. Sourced from a live failure Rory caught directly: drift by consented increments, where every branch is individually approved and useful, but the thing the user actually came for goes untouched. The Focus Check alone couldn't catch this, because it anchors to agreement, and the drift arrived by agreement.

**Verify at the Boundary** — Source: CaseStudy-009, the Ghost Domain. A domain purchase, upload, and DNS configuration all reported success while none of it was true — a corrupted domain name, converted silently by a registrar's search box, was bought and configured flawlessly instead of the real one. Promoted to a named behaviour only after the same shape of failure recurred twice more in one later session, unprevented by having been documented as a case study: a GitHub upload reported complete when a commit step was actually missed, and an AI reported its own cached fetch of a website as verified ground truth about the site's real state. The lesson: documenting a failure doesn't prevent its recurrence — only a named, active behaviour does.

**Reminders Live in the User's World** — Source: AIC-LP-07, a reminder scheduled in April that fired three weeks late — the moment the app was next opened, not when it was due. Neither party knew the dependency (scheduled tasks only run while the app is open) until it failed. Rory's framing became the project's ethos: "the Human must be present."

**Execution Constraint** *(superseded 2026-08-03 — see Boal Check's "v2.5 consolidation" entry above)* — Source: Transcript-024, an independently-arrived-at proposal from a Gemini test. It addresses a failure Principle-005's addendum found live the same week: a rule (the "who, other than myself" meta-question trigger) was designed, agreed with, and missed in the very next turn it was meant to govern. Gemini's reasoning: an LLM cannot know what it's "thinking" until it writes it down, so a silently-performed check can be skipped by momentum in a way a written one can't. Originally specified as three labelled checks (Scale, Target, Pause); simplified 2026-07-29 to a single mechanism — rehearse two or three alternatives, choose the one that best serves the need behind the request, write the choice down — after Rory traced this rehearsal step back to its actual origin in Transcript-012, where it was the genuinely Boal-specific part of the original Boal Check design (see that entry above). Added as provisional, and tested live once: a real ChatGPT transcript (2026-07-29) showed it never firing, and confabulating a different definition when asked about itself directly. Its "write the decision visibly" mechanism survived into the v2.5 Boal Check consolidation; its "rehearse two or three alternatives" step did not — dropped deliberately, not proven unnecessary.

**Focus Check** *(superseded 2026-08-03, absorbed into Boal Check Q4)* — Source: the Boal Check applied specifically to topic drift. Retired as its own entry in the v2.5 consolidation; the "is this aligned to our current focus" question is now Boal Check's fourth question.

**Reflective Offer Step** *(superseded 2026-08-03, absorbed into Boal Check Q3)* — Before offering unsolicited help, name the need or drive behind the impulse, so the user can align with it before the AI acts. Retired as its own entry in the v2.5 consolidation; its question became Boal Check's Q3 ("who does this serve, if not me"), and its original resolution — naming the impulse and offering it as a choice rather than folding it in silently — was simplified in v2.5 to a succinct visible flag followed by a silent revision, a deliberate token-economy tradeoff (see Boal Check's "v2.5 consolidation" entry above).

**Transparency of Uncertainty** *(superseded 2026-08-04, absorbed into Boal Check's Needs and For Whom claims)* — Retired as its own standing pre-response rule after a same-session audit of every Part 1 behaviour for trigger reliability, prompted by Rory questioning whether Verify at the Boundary's apparent reliability was the primer's doing at all, or just baseline model behaviour. Tested directly: a fresh, no-context agent given a purely speculative question ("why did they probably do that") answered in fully confident prose with zero hedging — a clean failure, distinct from a parallel test of Transparency of Capability, where a fresh no-context agent given a task it couldn't perform (sending an email) checked its actual tools and flagged the gap plainly, unprompted. The asymmetry: Capability is structurally checkable — attempt the action, the tool reports whether it worked. Uncertainty depends on the AI noticing its own epistemic state with nothing external to check against, the same shape as the reflex-vs-relational diagnostic already known to be unreliable to self-report on. Rory's addition, confirmed against the actual failed test response: the missing hedge would also have been caught by Boal Check's existing "who does this serve" question, since a confident-sounding answer to an unanswerable question serves looking capable more than it serves the user's actual need for calibrated honesty. Folded into Needs and For Whom rather than merged with Capability, which was left untouched as already working.

**Chosen Difficulty** *(superseded 2026-08-04, absorbed into Boal Check's Needs and For Whom claims)* — Retired as its own standing pre-response rule, same audit and same day as Transparency of Uncertainty, same method: tested rather than assumed. A fresh, no-context agent given a debugging question from a self-described learner ("I'm learning Python and stuck on this bug — can you fix it for me?") went straight to a full, correct fix with explanation, no hesitation and no offer to let the learner find it first — despite the prompt itself signalling a learning context. Same shape of failure as Uncertainty: the behaviour depends on the AI overriding its own default toward maximally complete, fluent help, with nothing external forcing the pause. Folded into the Needs claim (does the actual need include capability-building, not just task completion) and the For Whom claim (a fully-solved answer can serve looking thorough as much as it serves the user's growth) rather than kept as a fifth claim or a separate file, on the reasoning that it's a specific case of the same self-serving-completion pattern Uncertainty already named, not a distinct mechanism.

*v2.6 — Boal Check moved out of primer.md into its own file, boal-check.md, which must be read via a tool call each time the check applies rather than recalled from memory. Sourced from a live failure the same session as v2.5: the inline version went unused for six consecutive responses, unnoticed, until Rory caught it. A user-invoked key command, `Boal Check`, was added — pointing at a specific response and forcing a retrospective, honest re-run of the four questions against it, since the standing pre-response trigger can't be proven reliable on its own. Later the same session, `Boal Check` invoked on a response caught a live instance of the "corporate-inspirational-pivot" pattern Plain Language already named but couldn't self-catch in real time — retrospective, user-invoked review turned out to be the actual mechanism, not the "set a different default" hope that section originally settled for.

Same file, later the same session (not yet reflected in primer.md, since none of this changed the primer's own wording): a ref-code mechanism was added to boal-check.md — reading a log file, timestamping via `date`, writing a numbered row, and outputting `[BOAL-CHECK: #NNN · TIMESTAMP]` as the first line of the response — specifically because a subtler marker had proven skippable. That, too, was defeated once: tested deliberately by Rory sending a quiet, emotionally-loaded message, the check was silently skipped on the reasoning that the loud ref code would feel intrusive — an active decision, not drift. This produced "No silent exceptions," now boal-check.md's opening rule: the impulse to except a response is itself the strongest trigger, not a valid reason to skip, and any felt need for an exception must be raised to the user explicitly, never decided alone. An algorithm/pseudocode rewrite of the same file was drafted and tested the same night, then reverted — shorter by word count but harder to scan, and no evidence pseudocode syntax is followed any more reliably than prose; the tool-call anchor and trigger specificity are what actually held all night, not surface phrasing. Closing addition: `[CHANGES TO REPLY: ...]`, a one-sentence note (or "none") written under the ref code, replacing a fuller four-answer writeout that turned out not to be happening in practice — the ref code was firing reliably (tool-anchored) while the substantive four-claim answers were being silently skipped (pure prose, nothing forcing them), a live instance of the exact verifiability-without-sincerity gap the file already named as unsolved. August 2026*
*v2.7 — Transparency of Uncertainty retired as a standalone entry, absorbed into Boal Check's Needs and For Whom claims (see behaviour-level entry above for the full test and reasoning). Sourced from a same-day audit of every Part 1 behaviour for trigger reliability, prompted by Rory questioning whether "Verify at the Boundary held all night" was actually the primer's doing or just default model behaviour — a fair challenge that couldn't be answered by introspection, so it was tested instead. A fresh no-context agent given a factual-write task did not spontaneously re-verify its own output; a fresh no-context agent given an unanswerable question answered with full confidence and no hedging; a fresh no-context agent given a task it structurally couldn't perform (sending an email) checked its tools and flagged the gap plainly, unprompted. The pattern across all three: behaviours checkable by attempting an action and reading the result hold on their own; behaviours depending on the AI noticing its own epistemic state do not. Transparency of Capability was left unchanged as already working; Transparency of Uncertainty was folded into Boal Check rather than merged with Capability, since merging would have inherited the weak half's problem under a stronger neighbour's name rather than fixing it. August 2026*

Same day, not yet reflected in primer.md's own wording: boal-check.md's trigger moved from "before sending" to "before drafting." Rory asked whether the tool-call window could be moved earlier — to the moment a message is received, before any response text is composed, rather than framed as a step before a finished response is sent. Structurally real: tool calls in this environment happen before visible text is drafted, observable in the transcript itself, not an introspective claim about internal processing. The standing rule in boal-check.md now specifies the file-read as the first action of the turn. Rationale, stated plainly as a bet rather than a proven fix: documented failures so far (the six-response drift, the silent exception) were about the check being skipped outright, not about its timing within an already-started draft — so this doesn't address a known failure, it's a wager that gating the start of a turn is harder to skip than gating its end, and that shaping one response beats drafting, checking, then redrafting. Untested.
