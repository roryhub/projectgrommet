# CaseStudy-011 — Symptoms, Not the Source: The Soufflé Text Problem
*Logged 2026-08-05. Source: this session, while reviewing and editing llms.txt line by line.*

---

## The admission

While personally editing llms.txt's Core/Reference sections rather than directing the AI to do it, Rory named something directly: *"I didn't really know the structure of this project, what files were actually being used and when, if ever... This lack of a sense of ownership was contributing to my anxiety about the project and what was achievable. Because I now realise the current framework is operating piecemeal."*

Followed, unprompted, by the harder version of the same admission: *"I think my comment about editing the llms is worth capturing. It's me owning my own misstep in handing over responsibility to AI."*

## The counter-argument

In the same breath, Rory named a second, causal factor — not an excuse, a co-cause: *"AI's inclination to use verbose 'Souffle' text doesn't encourage a deep read by the user, thereby exacerbating the issue!"* A soufflé looks substantial and collapses on inspection — text that reads as thorough while carrying little that rewards a close, second look trains the reader not to give it one. If a user has learned, correctly, that most AI output doesn't reward deep reading, handing over responsibility isn't laziness — it's a rational response to the actual texture of what's usually on offer. The two causes compound: an AI defaults toward soufflé text, which discourages the deep read that would build ownership, which produces exactly the piecemeal, ungrounded sense of the project Rory named above.

## The broader realisation

Named separately, in direct connection to CON_05 (Foundations.md — *"Context is vital and AI rarely takes the time to work out the context before responding"*): *"I also realised... that we have been addressing the symptoms of context-disconnect and not the source. Especially that Needs-based communication — the thing that the AI has not really been implementing — is our current best tool in that regard."*

Checked directly against the two files this session, at Rory's request, before responding: the claim holds. Boal Check's four claims verify a response against context already assumed to be in hand — Needs, Energy, For Whom, and Focus are all checks on something already forming, not a mandate to gather more before drafting starts. Needs-based-communication.md's guessing syntax — *"offer one guess at a time, wait, let the user correct you"* — is structurally different: it is iterative, upstream, actual context-gathering, not verification of an assumed context after the fact. Most of what has been built this project addresses a downstream symptom of the completion-pull CON_05 named in July. The one mechanism that addresses the source directly is the one the AI has been least consistent about actually using.

## Why this matters

Chosen Difficulty (CON_09) exists because the Boiling Frog study found something counterintuitive: people who had AI available and chose not to use it outperformed people who never had the option at all — having the easy path and walking past it produced the capability, not the completed task. Rory's own act tonight — reading and editing llms.txt himself, line by line, rather than directing it — is that mechanism live, on the person who wrote the theory. The anxiety he named ("I will be found out") is the thing CON_09 predicts happens when ownership is claimed without the underlying engagement; naming it and then doing the engagement anyway, in the same session, is the fix demonstrating itself.

## What this suggests for the framework (not yet actioned)

Two open threads, deliberately not resolved here:

- **Soufflé text as a stake, not just a style note.** Plain Language over Performed Thoughtfulness currently frames verbosity as a sincerity signal (rule of three, corporate-inspirational register, and — as of tonight — negation-echo). This incident suggests a sharper, more consequential rationale worth adding: verbose text doesn't just read as performed, it actively discourages the deep read that builds a user's own ownership of their work. That's a different, higher-stakes argument than "it sounds insincere."
- **The guessing syntax may need to be more than a reference file consulted on request.** If it's the framework's actual best tool against the root cause CON_05 named, and it currently only gets read "at the invitation of your user" or when the primer directs it, that may be underselling its own importance relative to what this incident just demonstrated.
